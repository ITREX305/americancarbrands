When people think of American cars, they picture the F-150, the Mustang, a Corvette on a highway. But the full story spans 120 years and hundreds of marques — from Packard to Tesla, from the Tucker Torpedo to the Rivian R1T. This is the definitive guide.

The United States has produced more automobile brands than any country on earth. Since Ransom Olds launched his curved-dash Olds in 1901, Americans have started over 1,800 automotive companies. Most vanished within a decade. A few became legends. Today, around 38 active brands still build vehicles with American roots — and a new generation of EV startups is writing the next chapter.

America's Largest Automakers

The Big Three

3 companies · ~12M vehicles/year

Ford, General Motors, and Stellantis have dominated American automotive manufacturing since the 1920s. Together they account for the majority of new vehicles sold in the US annually — though their combined share has eroded from 90%+ in the 1960s to around 40% today.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked
Questions

What is the oldest American car brand?
+

The oldest surviving American car brand is Buick, founded in 1899. However, Oldsmobile was actually founded in 1897 — making it older — but it was discontinued in 2004. If we include only brands that are still actively producing vehicles today, Buick (1899) takes the title, followed closely by Cadillac (1902) and Ford (1903). The oldest American automaker as a company is General Motors, founded 1908, though it operates through brands older than the company itself.

Is Tesla considered an American car brand?
+

Yes — Tesla is unambiguously an American car brand. The company was founded in 2003 in San Carlos, California, is headquartered in Austin, Texas, and manufactures vehicles in Fremont, California and Austin, Texas. It trades on NASDAQ under the ticker TSLA. While CEO Elon Musk was born in South Africa and has global business interests, Tesla the company is American-incorporated, American-headquartered, and primarily American-manufactured. It is the largest American-born automaker by market capitalization.

Which American car brands are still active in 2026?
+

There are approximately 38 active American car brands in 2026. The major active brands include: Ford, Chevrolet, Dodge, Jeep, Ram, GMC, Cadillac, Lincoln, Buick, Chrysler (minimal), Tesla, Rivian, and Lucid. Performance and boutique brands include Shelby, Saleen, Hennessey, SSC North America, and Panoz. Several EV startups are in production or pre-production including Scout Motors, Canoo, Aptera, and Workhorse. See the full list of active American car brands →

Are Jeep and Dodge still American-owned?
+

This is nuanced. Jeep and Dodge are American-origin brands with deep American heritage, but they are now owned by Stellantis — a multinational corporation incorporated in the Netherlands, formed by the 2021 merger of Fiat Chrysler Automobiles (FCA) and France's PSA Group. Stellantis has significant American operations and manufactures many vehicles in the US. So while the brands are American in origin and culture, the parent company is technically not purely American-owned. This is similar to how Jaguar and Land Rover are British brands owned by India's Tata Motors.

How many American car brands have gone out of business?
+

Over the history of the American automobile industry, more than 1,800 companies attempted to build cars — and the vast majority failed within a decade. Among major brands that had significant production runs, we track 47 notable defunct American car brands including Pontiac, Oldsmobile, Saturn, Packard, Studebaker, Plymouth, Mercury, AMC, Hudson, Nash, DeSoto, Tucker, DeLorean, and many more. The biggest wave of discontinuations came during GM's 2009 bankruptcy restructuring, when Pontiac, Saturn, and Hummer were all killed in a single year. Browse all defunct American car brands →

What American car brands are publicly traded stocks?
+

Several American car brands have publicly traded parent companies: Ford Motor Company (NYSE: F), General Motors (NYSE: GM) — which covers Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac — Stellantis (NYSE: STLA) — covering Dodge, Ram, Jeep, and Chrysler — Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA), Rivian (NASDAQ: RIVN), and Lucid Group (NASDAQ: LCID).
