Ford's Most Common Problems by Model

⚙️
PowerShift Transmission (2011–2016)
The dual-clutch PowerShift automatic used in Focus and Fiesta was Ford's worst reliability failure in recent decades. Shuddering, slipping, and eventual failure were widespread. Resulted in class-action settlement. Avoid any Focus or Fiesta with this transmission.
🌡️
EcoBoost Engine Issues (2013–2016)
Early EcoBoost engines had documented carbon buildup, oil consumption, timing chain issues, and in the 1.6L specifically, fire risk. These issues improved substantially in 2017+ model years. Reliability data strongly favors 2018+ EcoBoost products.
💻
SYNC Software Issues
Ford's early SYNC infotainment systems (2013–2017) had documented reliability and freezing issues. SYNC 3 (2018+) and SYNC 4 (2020+) are significantly more reliable. Factor infotainment reliability into model year selection.
🔌
F-150 Lightning 2022–2023
Ford's first-generation Lightning had documented battery and fire-related recalls in 2022–2023. Production was temporarily halted. 2024+ models resolved the specific issues, but verify recall completion on any used 2022–2023 Lightning.
🚙
Explorer 2011–2019 Issues
The 5th-generation Explorer (2011–2019) had documented rear wheel vibration issues, carbon monoxide ingestion complaints, and transmission problems. The 2020+ redesign resolved most issues — buy 2020+ if considering Explorer.
Ford Reliability by Era

Pre-2017 Ford EcoBoost products carry higher risk. Post-2017 Ford products are significantly more reliable across the lineup. This is the most actionable reliability heuristic for Ford buyers: model year matters more than model name.

The Ford Reliability Verdict

Ford's reliability picture is nuanced. The brand makes some of America's most reliable vehicles (Maverick, F-150 5.0L, Mustang GT, Bronco Sport) and some of its most problematic (early EcoBoost engines, PowerShift transmission, Explorer 5th gen). The consistent pattern: post-2018 Ford products are significantly more reliable than pre-2017 equivalents.

Consumer Reports consistently gives Ford a "Below Average" to "Average" brand reliability score — lower than Toyota and Honda, but competitive with Chevrolet and ahead of Stellantis/FCA brands. The brand's reliability trajectory is improving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ford a reliable car brand?
Ford has average to above-average reliability compared to all brands globally, and is among the more reliable domestic American brands. Some Ford models (F-150, Explorer 2020+, Bronco Sport) score well. Others (EcoBoost engines, PowerShift transmission) have documented issues that bring down the brand average.
What are the most reliable Ford vehicles?
F-150 with the 5.0L Coyote V8 or 2.7L EcoBoost (2018+), Maverick, Bronco Sport, and Explorer (2020+) are Ford's most reliable current models. The Mustang GT with the 5.0L Coyote is also known for strong long-term reliability.
What Ford vehicles should I avoid?
Avoid Ford vehicles with the PowerShift dual-clutch (Focus, Fiesta 2011–2016) — documented widespread transmission failures. Avoid F-150 Lightning 2022–2023 (fire recalls). Approach Edge and Escape 2013–2014 with caution due to documented powertrain issues.
Are Ford EcoBoost engines reliable?
It depends on the specific EcoBoost engine and production year. The 3.5L and 2.7L EcoBoost in the F-150 (2018+) are generally reliable. The 1.6L EcoBoost (2013–2016) had significant documented issues. The 1.5L EcoBoost (2015–2016) had oil dilution issues resolved in 2017+. EcoBoost reliability improved significantly in 2017 and later model years.
Does Ford have good warranty support?
Ford offers 3-year/36,000-mile bumper-to-bumper and 5-year/60,000-mile powertrain warranty. Extended warranty via Ford Extended Service Plan is available. Ford's dealer network is extensive — the largest truck dealer network in the US provides strong service accessibility.
