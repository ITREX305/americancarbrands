Overview

John Hennessey has spent three decades building the most extreme versions of American cars that physics will permit. The Venom GT held the production car speed record before the Chiron. The Venom F5 is his definitive statement — a ground-up original design targeting 300 mph with no turbos, no supercharger, no compromise.

The Fury engine is a Hennessey-original 6.6L V8 producing 1,817 hp naturally aspirated. To put this in context: the SSC Tuatara requires twin turbochargers to reach 1,750 hp. The Bugatti Chiron uses a W16 with four turbochargers for 1,578 hp. The Venom F5 surpasses both with a simpler two-valve-per-cylinder V8 that revs to 8,200 rpm on atmospheric pressure alone. This is an engineering achievement without contemporary parallel.

The carbon fiber body and monocoque weigh just 1,971 lbs — making the Venom F5 one of the lightest production hypercars ever built. At 1,817 hp divided by 1,971 lbs, the power-to-weight ratio is 0.92 hp per pound. The Bugatti Chiron manages 0.46 hp/lb. The difference in acceleration is not linear — it is exponential.

Wind tunnel testing and CFD analysis developed the aerodynamics specifically for 300+ mph stability. The active rear wing generates downforce at lower speeds and reduces drag at maximum velocity. The team has targeted sub-8-second quarter miles and verified top speed runs above 300 mph. With only 24 units total production, every F5 is guaranteed to appreciate significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the Venom F5 naturally aspirated at 1,817 hp?
The Fury 6.6L V8 achieves 1,817 hp through extremely high compression (15.1:1), titanium valvetrain, dry-sump lubrication, and precision port fuel injection tuned for maximum volumetric efficiency. It revs to 8,200 rpm where the combination of displacement and breathing efficiency accumulates into record power output — no turbos required.
Has the Venom F5 achieved 300 mph?
Hennessey has conducted high-speed runs and continues to pursue the 300 mph barrier. The car's aerodynamics and power output are validated for 300+ mph capability. Confirmed top speed runs are ongoing; check hennessey.com for current records.
How does the Venom F5 compare to a Koenigsegg?
The Venom F5 (1,817 hp, $2.1M naturally aspirated) surpasses the Koenigsegg Regera (1,500 hp, $1.9M) and Jesko Absolut (1,600 hp, $3.0M) on power. The Koenigsegg uses a twin-turbocharged V8 and electric hybrid system; the Venom F5's all-natural 1,817 hp is a more pure engineering statement.
Are all 24 Venom F5 units sold?
The initial 24-unit production run was pre-sold. Hennessey has indicated potential for additional production configurations (Revolution track variant). Contact Hennessey Performance directly for current availability.
Can the Venom F5 be driven on public roads?
Yes — the Venom F5 is street legal in the United States. It meets all Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards and DOT requirements. Practically, the car's performance capabilities far exceed what can be legally or safely used on public roads.
What maintenance does the Fury V8 require?
The Fury V8 requires full synthetic oil changes every 3,000 miles, valve adjustment checks every 10,000 miles, and a full engine inspection every 20,000 miles or annually. At high-performance use levels, Hennessey recommends factory service annually regardless of mileage.
Where can the Venom F5 be serviced?
Primary service is at Hennessey Performance in Sealy, Texas. Hennessey will travel to customer locations for scheduled maintenance. For owners outside the US, Hennessey has established relationships with select European and Asian service partners.
What is the Venom F5's fuel economy?
The EPA does not test vehicles in this production volume. Hennessey estimates approximately 9 mpg city / 15 mpg highway in conservative driving. At maximum performance, fuel consumption is measured in gallons per minute, not miles per gallon.
