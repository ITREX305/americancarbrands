Ownership Overview

Who Is the Ram 2500 For?

Buyers who regularly tow trailers over 10,000 lbs — fifth wheels, heavy equipment, multiple horse trailers. Diesel buyers who want the long-term Cummins durability advantage. Commercial operators who need HD capability with Ram's interior quality.

✅ STRENGTHS
  • ✓ Available 6.7L Cummins inline-6 diesel — 800 lb-ft torque, legendary durability
  • ✓ 20,000 lb tow rating (Cummins) — segment-competitive heavy-duty capacity
  • ✓ Ram interior quality advantage carries over from 1500 — best in HD segment
  • ✓ Coil-spring front suspension (independent) vs competitors' solid front axles
  • ✓ Strong Cummins diesel resale value for high-mileage commercial buyers
⚠ CONSIDERATIONS
  • ⚠ Cummins diesel adds approximately $10,000 to base price
  • ⚠ Ride is firm for non-towing daily use — body-on-frame HD dynamics
  • ⚠ Fuel economy (Hemi) is modest even for HD segment
  • ⚠ Less dealer network depth for commercial use vs Ford Super Duty
  • ⚠ Larger size makes urban maneuvering more challenging
Quick Specs: 11 city / 16 hwy (6.4L Hemi) · 15 city / 20 hwy (Cummins)  ·  Built in Saltillo, Mexico  ·  Parent: Stellantis  ·  NHTSA Recalls ↗

FAQ · Schema Marked Up

Ram 2500 — Common Questions

The most searched questions — answered with data.

The 6.7L Cummins adds approximately $10,000 over the Hemi V8. Advantages: 800 lb-ft torque vs 429 lb-ft (Hemi), better fuel economy (~19 mpg hwy vs ~16), stronger long-term durability record, and higher resale value for commercial buyers. The Cummins payback in fuel savings typically takes 5–8 years depending on mileage. For buyers who tow regularly at high loads and plan high mileage, the Cummins is widely considered worth it. For light-duty HD buyers, the Hemi is adequate.
Ram 2500 tow ratings: 6.4L Hemi — up to 17,540 lbs (conventional); 6.7L Cummins (standard output) — up to 18,210 lbs; 6.7L Cummins (high output) — up to 19,680 lbs. Fifth-wheel ratings with kingpin adapter: up to 20,000 lbs (Cummins HO). Specific configurations, cab styles, and options affect the final rating — always check the Ram towing guide for your specific build.
Ford F-250 advantages: 7.3L Godzilla V8 option, stronger commercial/fleet presence, wider dealer network. Ram 2500 advantages: significantly better interior quality at equivalent trims, Cummins diesel reputation for durability, independent front suspension improves ride. For commercial fleet buyers, F-250 ecosystem is broader. For owner-operators and those who value interior quality, Ram 2500 wins.
The Ram 2500 does not offer the same air suspension as the 1500. It uses a coil-spring independent front suspension and leaf-spring rear — a more conventional HD truck setup. The coil-spring front is an advantage over the solid front axle used by Ford Super Duty and GM HD, providing better ride quality for non-towing use.
The Ram 2500 has above-average reliability, particularly with the Cummins diesel engine which has a strong long-term durability record among HD trucks. Common areas to watch on high-mileage examples include the exhaust after-treatment system (DEF/DPF), transfer case, and U-joints. The Hemi V8 is proven and reliable for its intended use.
