01

Overview

The Cybertruck is Tesla's full-size electric pickup truck, delivered starting November 2023 after years of delays from the original 2019 reveal. Produced at Gigafactory Texas in Austin, it is the only production vehicle with a cold-rolled stainless steel exoskeleton — a manufacturing constraint that contributed to its development timeline.

Lineup Updated — Early 2026

Tesla restructured the Cybertruck lineup in early 2026: RWD ($79,990) has been phased out as the base trim. The new base is Dual Motor AWD at $69,990 — a significant price reduction. The Cyberbeast dropped from $99,990 to $89,990. Verify current trim availability and pricing at tesla.com before ordering.

Two trims in active production for 2026: the Cybertruck AWD (dual-motor all-wheel drive, $69,990, ~325 mi EPA) and the Cyberbeast (tri-motor AWD, $89,990, 301 mi EPA). The original RWD ($79,990) has been phased out as the entry variant.

Range

EPA range figures are measured under standardized laboratory conditions. Real-world range — especially highway driving, cold weather, or towing — is significantly lower. The Cybertruck's high curb weight (~6,600–6,800 lbs) accelerates range degradation at speed compared to lighter EVs.

Towing Range Penalty is Severe

At maximum tow loads, expect a 40–55% range reduction — leaving as few as 130–185 miles of usable range on the AWD base trim. For long-distance towing, plan Supercharger stops every 100–130 miles as a conservative baseline.

06

Charging

The Cybertruck uses NACS (SAE J3400) — the native Tesla connector — giving it direct access to the Supercharger network without adapters. 45,000+ stalls in the US, with consistent uptime. The vehicle charges at up to 250 kW DC fast.

Supercharger Access — Native

All Tesla vehicles including Cybertruck access Superchargers natively. For trips exceeding 200 miles when towing, pre-route Supercharger stops using the in-car navigation — it automatically calculates charging windows.

Vehicle-to-Home (V2H) / Powershare

The Cybertruck includes Powershare — a bidirectional power export feature. It can output up to 11.5 kW via onboard 240V outlets. This allows the Cybertruck to power home circuits or job site equipment. A Tesla Powerwall or whole-home Powershare gateway enables whole-home backup during outages.

Stainless Steel Corrosion Note

Early Cybertruck owners in humid/coastal environments reported surface rust spotting on the stainless exoskeleton. Tesla has stated this is surface contamination, not structural corrosion, and can be addressed with specific cleaning procedures. Not a structural safety issue — but worth monitoring in saltwater-adjacent environments.

Consult Consumer Reports, NHTSA, and real-world owner forums (r/TeslaCybertruck, TeslaMotorsClub) for current reliability data. This vehicle is in its second year of production as of 2026 — make purchasing decisions accordingly.

Recalls

NHTSA · 2024 · ~3,878 Cybertrucks
Accelerator Pedal Insert May Dislodge
The accelerator pedal exterior insert could detach and slide under the brake pedal, causing unintended acceleration or difficulty braking. This is a physical hardware defect. Physical repair at a Tesla Service Center is required — this cannot be resolved via OTA update. Check your VIN immediately at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

A software error could cause front windshield wipers to fail during operation, reducing driver visibility and increasing crash risk. Resolved via over-the-air software update. Confirm your software version is current in the Tesla app.

FAQ

Does the Cybertruck qualify for the federal EV tax credit?
No. Both Cybertruck trims exceed the $80,000 MSRP cap for trucks under the Inflation Reduction Act. The AWD base is $69,990 and the Cyberbeast $89,990. While the AWD trim is under the $80,000 truck MSRP cap on price alone, the Cybertruck currently does not qualify due to IRA battery sourcing requirements. Neither trim qualifies for the $7,500 federal EV tax credit as of March 2026. Verify current rules at fueleconomy.gov/feg/taxcenter.do before purchasing.
What is the Cybertruck's real-world towing range?
Expect 40–55% range reduction at maximum tow loads. The AWD base at ~325 miles EPA may deliver approximately 130–185 miles when towing near max capacity at highway speeds. The Cyberbeast at 301 miles EPA may deliver 110–170 miles under similar conditions. These figures vary significantly by load weight, driving speed, and terrain grade. For any trip over 150 miles while towing, plan Supercharger stops in advance using the in-car navigation.
What is the Cybertruck's bed vault length?
The Cybertruck vault (bed) is 6 feet long (72 inches) and does not fold or extend. It includes a powered tonneau cover standard. It is shorter than a full-size truck long bed (typically 8 feet) but comparable to standard-bed half-tons. The vault is sealed and water-resistant.
Is the Cybertruck's stainless steel body rust-proof?
No, not completely. The 300-series stainless steel is highly corrosion-resistant but not impervious. Early owners in humid, coastal, or road-salt environments reported surface rust spotting. Tesla has stated this is surface contamination from iron particles in the air and can be addressed with stainless steel cleaning products. It is not structural corrosion. Tesla recommends specific care procedures available at tesla.com.
What Cybertruck trims are available in 2026?
Two active production trims as of March 2026: Cybertruck AWD ($69,990, ~325 mi EPA est., dual-motor AWD, ~4.1s 0–60) and Cyberbeast ($89,990, 301 mi EPA, tri-motor AWD, 2.6s 0–60). The original single-motor RWD ($79,990) has been phased out. The Foundation Series was an early-delivery premium configuration — no longer the primary offering. Verify current trim availability at tesla.com.
Can the Cybertruck power my home?
Yes, via Powershare. The Cybertruck outputs up to 11.5 kW through onboard 240V outlets. A dedicated Powershare home gateway or integration with Tesla Powerwall 3 allows whole-home backup during power outages. The vehicle's battery can run an average home for approximately 3–4 days depending on consumption. This requires a home installation — consult a licensed electrician and Tesla's Powershare documentation.
